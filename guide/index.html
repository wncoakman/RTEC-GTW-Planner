What the app is, and what it isn't

The Job Builder is an offline worksheet that produces one proposal-ready document set per property. It does not talk to SingleOps. Nothing it makes is an import file — the ZIP is the source document a person reads while typing the proposal. Knowing that is the difference between a report Conor can work straight down, and one he has to interview you about.

Before the first job

Arborist

Ten minutes, once, on wifi — not in the driveway of your first estimate.

  1. Open the app on wifi. https://wncoakman.github.io/RTEC-GTW-Planner/ in Safari (iPhone/iPad) or Chrome (Android). The first load caches the whole app and its libraries, which is what makes it work later with no signal at all.
  2. Add it to the home screen. Share → Add to Home Screen. It then opens full-screen like an app, and keeps its own saved jobs.
  3. Check the OneDrive app is signed in to your RTEC account, and that you can see the shared GTW Job Builder Exports folder. If you can't, that's Melinda.
  4. Learn the two header buttons. ↻ checks for an update — it saves the open job first, installs, and reloads; an amber dot means one is waiting. 🐞 opens a pre-filled email to report a problem, with the device details and recent errors already in it.
Why it matters Nothing in this app is on a server. Jobs live in the phone's own storage until you export a ZIP, and that export is the only permanent copy.

The walkthrough

Arborist

Recorded on the demo job above. Tap any screen to play it. Four steps across the top of the app, in order, and you can jump back to any of them at any time.

Step 1 · Client & Site
STEP 1

Client & Site

Seven fields, and they key everything downstream: the PDF title banner, the ZIP filename, every photo label and every tree ID.

  • SingleOps # first, before any photo. Tree and task IDs are built from it — 41827-1, 41827-1a. Leave it blank and the export falls back to Tree #1 and 1a with no job reference anywhere, and someone renames seven files at the office.
  • Client name in house format: Last, First — couples are Last, First and First. That's the string Conor matches against the SingleOps client record.
  • City is forced to capitals on export. Type it however you like.
  • Arborist / Sales Rep is a type-or-pick list. Your last pick becomes the default on your next job, and it becomes the Sales Rep on the proposal — a job without one fails the office's checks.
  • Date is the inspection date, not the work date. It lands in the title banner and in both filenames.
Step 2 · Tree 1, photo and GPS
STEP 2 · A

Add a tree: photo, then a fix, then the facts

Work in that order and you never have to walk back. Each tree takes as many photos as you want.

  • Capture GPS averages for about fifteen seconds — it drops outliers and weights the readings by accuracy, and shows you the count as it goes. Stand at the trunk and hold still. Stop & save ends it early if you've got a good number.
  • Read the ± before you move on. The demo settled at ±4 m from 22 readings. Worse than about ±8 m under canopy is normal — fix it on the satellite view rather than accepting it.
  • DBH in inches, digits only. It prints on the SingleOps GTW work order, so the crew reads your number on the day.
  • Write the location for someone who has never been there. “Side yard, between house and neighbor's garage, under the service drop” gets a crew to the right stem. “Front yard” does not.
Step 2 · Photo markup
STEP 2 · B

Mark up the photo

Tap a thumbnail to open it full size, then Edit. Select, Draw, Shapes (box, ellipse, line, arrow), Text and Magnify, with six colours, a line-width slider and Undo / Clear / Delete.

  • The original is never overwritten. The ZIP ships both 41827-1-original.jpg and 41827-1-edited.jpg, so the annotated copy can go to the client and the clean one stays on file.
  • Three marks is usually enough — one shape on the thing, one arrow to it, one short label. Anything more and the photo stops being evidence.
  • Keep labels away from the edge. Text placed near the left or right border gets clipped when the image is flattened. Drop it well inside the frame, then drag it into place with the Select tool.
  • On a phone the Text tool opens the keyboard to type the label; tap a placed label again to edit it, and use the Font slider to size it.
Step 2 · Work tasks
STEP 2 · C

Work tasks: 1a, 1b, 2a…

Every task under a tree is lettered. That label is the same one you'll see on the report line, on the photo filename and on the proposal line it becomes.

  • Est. Man-Hours is the one number the office cannot invent. SingleOps starts each GTW group from a default crew-hours figure and the estimator adjusts it to your number. A blank here means a guess there, and a weekly report flags GTW jobs whose hours were never changed.
  • “Optional proposal item?” Included, Recommended or Optional. It prints as a tag on the line and becomes a Recommended or Optional proposal line in SingleOps — which is where add-ons belong, not in the notes.
  • Split Job means a separate return visit, numbered on from the first: 41827-2. Use it for the stump grind that happens another day. Don't use it for work the same crew does the same morning.
  • The task list is type-or-pick. Anything new you type is remembered on your device for next time — useful, but see the note under Limits about catalog drift.
  • Tasks carry their own photos (41827-1b), separate from the tree's.
Step 2 · Satellite fine-tune
STEP 2 · D

Put the pin on the trunk

Fine-tune opens the aerial. Drag the map until the crosshair sits on the stem, pinch or scroll to zoom. The dashed ring is where GPS put it; the other numbered pins are the rest of this property's inventory, so you can see the whole layout as you work.

  • This needs a connection. On a dead-signal property the button won't help — capture GPS, finish the job, and fine-tune from the truck.
  • Saving replaces the fix. The summary then reads “placed on satellite” and the ± figure is cleared. That's correct: a hand-placed pin has no measured accuracy.
  • This is what makes the site map in the report worth printing. A map of ±10 m pins on a quarter-acre lot tells the crew nothing.
Step 3 · Logistics
STEP 3

Logistics, safety, access & permits

This screen is the one the crew actually reads on the day — nearly all of it lands on the 5×7 notecard.

  • Equipment pickers feed two things: the Required Equipment list and the Tags chips at the end of the report. Crane and Spyder Lift are priced items and add to the total; everything else is a need, not a charge.
  • The work plan is an order of operations, not a description. Where each truck parks, which tree comes down first and why, the access route, drop zones, ground protection, what happens to the wood.
  • Safety wants three specifics: which tree is in the wire, what class of conductor, and what Pepco has to do — drop, sleeve, or de-energize.
  • Tick a permit, then open “Add details”. The placeholder tells you what's wanted: ticket number, who applies, dates, hours allowed. A ticked box with no detail is a phone call someone has to make later.
  • Four note fields map one-for-one onto SingleOps — Crew, Client, Internal, Scheduling. Keep the four together under about 1,000 characters; longer notes fail the QuickBooks sync.
Step 4 · Generate
STEP 4

Generate the bundle

One button for the normal case. The header line is a sanity check on your own arithmetic — four inventory entries, $6,875.00.

  • Generate while you still have signal. The satellite site map is built at this moment, from live imagery. Offline, the report falls back to a drawn diagram — usable, but not the same thing.
  • Tap “PDF Report only” as well. It drops a loose copy of the report next to the ZIP, so the office can read it on a phone without unzipping anything. Thirty seconds that saves a phone call.
  • Expect a large file. This one came to 8 MB with seven photos. On a weak signal, start the OneDrive upload and let it finish before you drive off.
Header · Jobs on this device
ANY TIME

Jobs on this device

Every job you start stays on the phone. Search by client, SingleOps # or city; Copy duplicates one as a starting point for a similar property; New Job never discards the one you were on.

  • The footer shows what you're using. The demo job alone is 10 MB, almost all of it photos.
  • Delete is permanent and there is no backup anywhere. Export first, every time.
  • Clearing Safari's website data wipes unexported jobs. So does losing the phone. The ZIP in OneDrive is the record; the phone is scratch paper.

Getting it off the phone

Arborist

Do this before you leave the property, or at the latest before the end of the day. A job that only exists on your phone does not exist.

iPhone & iPad

  1. Tap Generate & Download ZIP Bundle, then PDF Report only. Safari saves both to Downloads.
  2. Tap the downloads arrow in the Safari address bar, then the file name. It opens in Files.
  3. Touch and hold the ZIP → Share → Save to Files → OneDrive, and pick the folder below. Repeat for the loose PDF.
  4. Wait for the OneDrive tick. On a weak signal it queues — leave the app open until it clears.

Android

  1. Same two taps in Chrome; the files land in Downloads.
  2. Open Files (or the notification), long-press the ZIP → Share → OneDrive.
  3. Choose the folder below and confirm the upload.
  4. Check the OneDrive app shows it synced, not pending.

Back at the office: building the proposal

Conor

Open the report PDF and work top to bottom. It was built in this order on purpose. Nothing in the ZIP imports — every line is typed, so the goal is to type it once, correctly.

Job settings first

  • Operation GTW, job type One-Off, Per Visit invoicing
  • Sales Rep = the arborist on the report (Ziggy Zartman)
  • Terms Due on receipt and the Tax Location set
  • Visit Location mapped from the Google suggestion

Before you send

  • Every tree line has Tree #, Species and DBH
  • Every group has crew hours that aren't zero and aren't the default
  • Equipment is on Equipment items, not in the notes
  • Add-ons are Recommended or Optional lines
  • 50% deposit on scheduling — unless the Customer Type is Commercial, Municipal or Government
  • A payment arrangement is on file and the client has no balance-due Notice
  • Sent with a Proposal-type template, and the sent PDF saved
  • The ZIP and the report PDF are attached to the job

What this doesn't do

Worth saying plainly, so nobody waits for a sync that isn't coming.

  • There is no integration. No API call, no import template, no field mapping. Every tree, task, hour and price is retyped into SingleOps by a person. The app's whole design — the report's section order, the 41827-1a photo labels — is about making that retyping fast and unambiguous, not about avoiding it.
  • The task list is not the item catalog. “Prune - Clearance (Structure/Utility)” is this app's vocabulary. Typing it into a SingleOps item search will eventually create near-duplicate items. Pick from the existing groups; only Melinda creates new items, only Conor creates new tags.
  • Nothing leaves the device on its own. No cloud account, no sync, no backup. The exported ZIP is the only copy that survives a wiped phone.
  • GPS accuracy is whatever the phone reports. The app averages and shows you the number, but nothing stops a ±15 m fix landing in the GeoJSON. Fine-tune on satellite, or say so.
  • Site-map labels crowd on tight lots. With four pins inside 30 m the species names overlap, as they do on the demo map. The numbered pins still read — use those and the report's location text.
  • Satellite imagery needs signal at generate time. Offline you get a drawn diagram instead, which is honest but much less useful to a crew.

Stuck, or something's wrong?
Use the 🐞 button in the app header — it fills in the device, the app build and the recent errors for you. For anything about the proposal itself, ask Conor; for a missing item, group or folder, ask Melinda.
